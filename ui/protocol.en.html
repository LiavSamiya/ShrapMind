Ferroxidase Bench Protocol

Three questions, three outputs: which residues in the enzyme respond to the pH a corroding fragment creates around itself, how that design becomes a DNA sequence you can order, and what to do at the bench — in an order where the cheapest experiment that can kill the route comes first.

The short answers

First, because two of the three questions were whether this is possible at all

Can you produce a DNA sequence that expresses the protein?

Yes, and it is arithmetic rather than research.

A protein sequence plus a codon table plus a constraint set gives a synthesisable gene. The output below is 576 real bases encoding human H ferritin, which passes a back-translation check against a standard codon table built independently of the one the tool uses. What this does not mean: that the protein folds, assembles, is active against a fragment, or is safe. Reverse translation is mechanical, so it carries every uncertainty the design carries — no more and no less.

Can you find the pH-sensitive sites in an existing enzyme?

Yes, and for a metal fragment the question is sharper than it first looks.

A side chain responds to a pH change only if its pKa lies between the two values. The usual framing — assay pH versus tissue pH — is the wrong span here. A corroding iron surface acidifies its own microenvironment, through Fe(II) hydrolysis, so the enzyme does not meet tissue at 7.4. It meets something closer to 5.5, and that is the span this analysis uses.

Who this protocol is for, after the premise was checked. The product assumed that making a fragment smaller relieves the pressure on the nerve beside it, and nothing in the project had tested that. In 26 shrapnel-injured nerves followed a median 33 months, the presence of foreign particles predicted a worse outcome — 28% pain improvement with them against 67% without — and nerve grafting beat neurolysis 100% to 47% (PMID 24898760). About 55% of shrapnel nerve injuries carry the fragment inside the nerve (PMID 42006932). So this is for fragments pressing on an intact nerve where surgery is refused or riskier than the fragment — not for a fragment in an injured nerve, where the evidence says graft, and never as a reason to delay surgery that is possible.

The reaction disables the enzyme that is supposed to manage it.

His65 is the catalytic histidine of ferritin's di-iron ferroxidase centre. Across pH 7.4 to 5.5 it goes from 4% protonated to 76%. Over the same span 41 of 69 titratable residues in the chain respond, against 13 across ferritin's own cytosolic range, which is the control on the method.

So dissolving the iron acidifies the surface, and the acidified surface protonates the active site of the enzyme placed there to oxidise that iron. Neither the team's clinical review nor the carrier document states this feedback, and it is not a subtle effect — it is the difference between a histidine that is almost entirely neutral and one that is mostly charged.

Part A — computational analysis

The deciding column is priority: ionisation change multiplied by proximity to the site. A residue has to be both responsive and near; either alone is not a lead. Distance is measured from the atoms that carry the charge rather than from the alpha carbon, because what couples a titratable residue to catalysis is where its proton sits. Note how the ranking is dominated by histidine and not by the four glutamates that actually coordinate the iron: Glu at pKa 4.2 is fully ionised at both ends of this span and does not respond at all.

What this table is not. These are model pKa values in water, not computed ones. A buried or metal-coordinated side chain can shift by several units, and in a di-iron centre that shift is large and is precisely the question. The table is a shortlist to hand to a real calculation — PROPKA, H++ or constant-pH MD — and not a substitute for one.

One piece of good news

Ferritin's theoretical isoelectric point is 5.18, with a net charge of -11.1 at pH 7.4. That is comfortably away from tissue pH, so the colloidal-stability problem that would afflict a protein near its pI does not apply here. For an injected depot that is one fewer formulation obstacle, and it is worth stating because the engine flags the opposite case elsewhere.

Two things that make this construct easier than it could have been. 1FHA carries no SSBOND record, so there is no disulfide and an ordinary BL21(DE3) cytoplasm will do — a thermophilic cutinase considered earlier needed a disulfide-competent strain. And ferritin is human, cytosolic and self-assembling: no signal peptide to trim, no metal cofactor to mature, no maturation operon. Only 1 codon had to move off the preferred choice to clear the restriction sites.

Why this is not the hydrogenase. The strongest lead in the metal arm is the secreted [NiFe] hydrogenase that oxidises Fe(0) on contact (PMID 30310166), and it is not what this protocol expresses. A [NiFe] hydrogenase is a two-subunit metalloenzyme that needs its whole hyp maturation machinery, a maturation protease, nickel loading and anaerobic handling throughout. You cannot order the gene and express it. Ferritin can be made this month; the hydrogenase is a project, and stage 7 below is the cheap test that decides whether it is worth starting.

Part C — the protocol

Seven stages, ordered so the cheapest experiment that can kill the route comes first

The ordering is the argument. Three of these stages need no protein at all, and one of them can end the programme — so they come before any expression work, not after it.

  1. Which steel is it2 daysno protein

    Fragment composition is an inclusion criterion, not something to discover afterwards. Carbon steel rusts and keeps rusting; stainless grows a chromium oxide film that is dense, adherent and self-healing, and the entire route stops working against it. Deferoxamine is selective for Fe(III) and does not lift Cr₂O₃. Ball bearings packed into a device are often stainless, and a radiograph does not tell them apart.

    1. XRF or SEM-EDS on every retrieved fragment available. Record iron, chromium, nickel, manganese.
    2. Chromium at or above about 10.5% means the fragment is outside this route.
    3. Record nickel too. It is released by dissolution, the chelator does not capture it, and metals.released_elements() now reports it by element rather than folding it into the iron.
    4. Culture the retrieved fragment, and record antibiotic cover and tetanus status. A chronically retained fragment is a contaminated foreign body, and a protein-rich hydrogel injected beside a colonised one is also a growth medium — biofilm additionally blocks the surface the chelator has to reach, which makes this a potency question and not only a safety one.
    Checkpoint. A composition, in percent, for every coupon used downstream. Coupons for the later stages must match the fragments the product is aimed at, not whatever steel the stores happen to hold.

  2. Does it dissolve at all, without an enzyme21 daysno protein · this gates everything

    This is the number nobody has measured, and the whole arm rests on it. Every corrosion rate the engine quotes comes from the biodegradable-implant literature and describes iron corroding on its own. What a chelator adds is unmeasured.

    1. Carbon-steel coupons of known surface area, in PBS at pH 7.4 and 37 °C, under air.
    2. Four arms: buffer alone; DFO at 1 mM; ascorbate at 1 mM; DFO plus ascorbate. The combination is there because ligand-promoted and reductive dissolution are reported to act synergistically on iron oxide (PMID 25965980), with ascorbate alone doing nothing in air.
    3. Measure released iron by ferrozine assay at days 1, 3, 7, 14, 21, and mass loss gravimetrically at the end.
    4. Report micrometres of penetration per year, so the result drops straight into the engine's timeline in the unit it already uses.
    Checkpoint, and the first stop decision. At the fastest rate anyone has reported, a 20% debulk of a 0.8 mm fragment takes about eight weeks. If the measured chelated rate is no better than the unchelated one, the chemistry does not work and no enzyme repairs that — which is why this costs a few hundred dollars and runs before anything else.

  3. Does the surface acidify3 daysno protein

    The computational finding above predicts that the reaction creates the pH that disables the enzyme. It is a prediction from model pKa values, and it is cheap to test directly.

    1. A pH microelectrode positioned at the coupon surface, or a ratiometric fluorescent pH probe imaged across the diffusion layer.
    2. Record the surface pH in each of the four arms from stage 2, since the chelator changes the speciation and may change the pH with it.
    Checkpoint. A number for the surface pH. If it sits near 7.4, the feedback loop is not real and stage 4 can be run at tissue pH alone, which halves it. If it sits near 5.5, the enzyme has to work at that pH and the engineering target is defined.

  4. Expression and purification5 days

    1. BL21(DE3) is sufficient — no disulfide, no cofactor maturation. Induce with 0.2 mM IPTG and shift to 18 °C overnight.
    2. IMAC on Ni-NTA. Use a nickel-free alternative if possible, or strip and dialyse exhaustively: this protein's entire function is binding metal ions, and an IMAC column is a metal-loading step.
    3. Endotoxin depletion — polymyxin-B affinity or anion exchange, with LAL as a release test. Absent from the first draft of this protocol; nothing downstream can enter an animal without it.
    4. SEC. Ferritin is a 24-mer of about 480 kDa and should run as one large, well-resolved peak; a monomer peak means the cage did not assemble.
    5. Iron-free the preparation before any assay. Apoferritin is the starting material, and a protein that arrives part-loaded gives an activity reading that is partly its own history.
    Checkpoint. One peak at the 24-mer position by SEC, and iron below detection by ferrozine. Native PAGE or TEM confirms the cage if SEC is ambiguous.

  5. The central measurement: ferroxidase activity against pH2 daysthis is the experiment

    1. Buffer series at matched ionic strength spanning 5.0 to 7.5 — MES below 6.5, HEPES above — because the span that matters is the one stage 3 measured, not the one a textbook assay uses.
    2. Add Fe(II) under air and follow Fe(III) formation at 305 nm, or follow the oxo-bridged intermediate at 650 nm. Run a no-enzyme control in every buffer: Fe(II) autoxidises on its own, and the rate is itself strongly pH dependent, so the uncatalysed background is not a constant that can be subtracted once.
    3. Define the unit before the first reading: one micromole of Fe(II) oxidised per minute at 37 °C, per milligram of protein by A280. An undefined unit is how a number loses its provenance.
    4. Add a dynamic light scattering read per buffer — the samples are already prepared, and it shows whether the cage survives the acid end.
    Checkpoint, and the real question. The ratio of enzymatic to uncatalysed rate, at each pH. If ferritin adds little over autoxidation at 7.4, its value is storage and not oxidation — which simplifies the programme enormously, because then this is a chelation problem with a protein-shaped container. If the ratio collapses below pH 6.5, the His65 prediction is confirmed and the engineering target is a ferroxidase that works at the surface pH.

  6. The mutant as a control1 day

    H65A removes the catalytic histidine. It is not a therapeutic candidate — it is the control that proves the signal is the ferroxidase site rather than iron binding to the protein surface, or autoxidation catalysed non-specifically by any protein.

    1. Same assay, pH 7.4 and the stage 3 surface pH, matched loading by A280.
    2. Alongside: no enzyme, heat-denatured ferritin, and an equal mass of bovine serum albumin.
    Checkpoint. H65A should approach the no-enzyme rate. If BSA also accelerates, what is being measured is surface catalysis by protein in general and not ferritin.

  7. Conditional: is the hydrogenase route worth starting1 weekgate, not a build

    The strongest lead in this arm acts on metallic Fe(0) directly, which is the one step ferritin cannot do — ferritin handles the ion after something else has released it. But both source organisms are strict anaerobes and tissue is oxygenated, so the first experiment is not expression, it is survival.

    1. Obtain a commercial O₂-tolerant [NiFe] hydrogenase, or spent medium from a Methanococcus maripaludis culture following the cell-free preparation (PMID 25900658).
    2. Measure residual activity after exposure to air at the dissolved oxygen tension of subcutaneous tissue, sampled over minutes to hours.
    3. In parallel, measure hydrogen evolution from a steel coupon. The mass balance says a 20% debulk of a 50 mg fragment releases 4.5 mL of H₂, inside a closed fibrotic capsule. Whether it accumulates or diffuses away is a measurement, and it has never been made.
    Checkpoint. If residual activity is minutes, the route needs an O₂-tolerant hydrogenase from Ralstonia or Aquifex and becomes a protein-engineering programme with a known starting point. If it is hours, it is the strongest thing in this arm and deserves the maturation work.

Stop decisions

Set in advance, because once you have seen the data it is too late

What halts the route and what continues it.
StageResultDecision
1Fragments are stainless Stop for those fragments. Not a tuning problem. Narrow the indication to carbon steel and say so.
2Chelated rate no better than unchelated Stop. The chemistry does not work and no enzyme repairs it. This is the cheapest way to learn it.
2Rate implies more than ten carrier placements Continue, but the indication changes: repeat injection beside a nerve is a different clinical proposition from one.
3Surface pH near 7.4 Good news. Drop the acid arm of stage 5 and the pH engineering with it.
5Enzymatic rate close to autoxidation Ferritin's value is capture, not catalysis. Reframe as a chelation product and stop calling it an enzyme therapy.
6BSA accelerates as much as ferritin Stop. The assay is measuring protein surfaces, not a ferroxidase.
7Hydrogenase inactivated in minutes under air Do not begin the maturation work. Revisit only with an O₂-tolerant enzyme.
What the mass balance already fixes, whatever the bench finds. For a 50 mg carbon-steel fragment, full capture needs 92 mg of ferritin — 1.8× the fragment's own mass — and a 20% debulk needs about 98 mg of deferoxamine, because DFO binds Fe(III) one ion per molecule. Neither number depends on any rate, and no improvement in the enzyme changes either. They are the arithmetic the formulation has to fit inside.

What is still missing

This register came out of reading the whole project as a biologist would, and it lives in code because a gap mentioned in conversation is a gap that disappears. Neither of the team's two feasibility documents raises a single item on it — which is not a criticism of either, since a document answers the question it set out to answer. These fall between them.

Eight have since closed. Four closed in code — the mass balance now runs element by element and reports the 0.20 mg a 20% debulk releases that nothing captures; demands sum across a patient's fragments instead of assuming one; the enzyme dose is arithmetic waiting on a single measurement; and there is now a named way to tell in a living subject whether anything is happening. Three closed because the protocol itself changed. And one closed by reading the literature, which is the one worth reading first.

The one that can invalidate the others. The whole product rests on the assumption that making a fragment smaller reduces the pressure on the nerve beside it, and no source anywhere in this project supports it. If the pressure comes from the contracting fibrotic capsule rather than from the fragment's bulk, debulking solves the wrong problem and no amount of enzyme engineering repairs that. Surgeons have the data to answer it. It is a reading task, not an experiment, and it should be settled before the pitch rather than during the questions.

How to reproduce. python -m tools.design_construct --target hFTH1 --uniprot P02794 --mutate H65A --offset 1 then python -m tools.build_protocol, which emits this page and its Hebrew counterpart from one run so the two cannot disagree. The structure is verified on every build: if any of the seven ferroxidase residues is not what it should be, the build fails rather than publishing.

Research prototype. Not a medical device, not a clinical decision aid, and not validated for use in a person or an animal. This is bench work on purified protein and steel coupons; it is not an animal study design, and that would need ethics approvals which are not part of this document.
