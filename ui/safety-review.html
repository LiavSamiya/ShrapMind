Will the catalyst attack the patient?

A cross-reference of 505 curated enzymes against ten fragment materials, asking two questions the gate battery originally conflated: what reaction are we running, and what else can this particular protein do.

Evidence snapshot 2026-10-27 PAZy · UniProtKB · BRENDA 2026.1 505 proteins · 396 observations

The finding that governs everything else

Why most runs end in a refusal

Not one turnover number in the knowledge base was measured on a solid fragment.

Of 266 kinetic measurements across 505 experimentally-verified enzymes, 180 were made on chromogenic model esters, 44 on soluble oligomers, 42 record no substrate at all. None on the real material. Only 8 enzymes have a kcat of any kind, and none has a measured half-life.

So every rate this system can compute is an extrapolation across substrates. It is reported with a band wide enough to say so, and the provenance gate refuses to let it be called a prediction.

The eighth entry on that list is worth naming on its own. Proteinase K carries a kcat of 3654 s−1, the highest in the dataset — measured on casein, milk protein. Fed into a surface-erosion model without checking what it was measured on, that single number predicts spectacular degradation of polylactide. It is a protease measured on a protein.

Two questions, not one

The gap this review opened

The original gate battery asked whether the bond class being attacked occurs in human tissue. For polylactide the answer is no: PLA hydrolysis is aliphatic-ester chemistry, and human structural protein is held together by peptide bonds. Every protease in the dataset passed that check.

But the question was asked about the reaction specification, not about the protein. All four peptidases in the warehouse are retrieved as PLA candidates, and their own catalytic activity is on peptide bonds — which is what collagen, elastin, fibronectin and laminin are made of. An enzyme that digests the tissue holding the wound together is worse than leaving the fragment in place.

Graded, not binary

A screen that blocked everything would be useless. Severity tracks the consequence: loss of structural integrity is not the same as local irritation.

The routes whose evidence holds, and why they still fail

An earlier version of this page said polyamide was the only material that cleared the provenance gate, and that NylB and NylC were the only two enzymes anywhere in the warehouse with a turnover number on a genuine oligomer of their own target. Rebuilding the figures from the engine corrected both claims, and the correction is worth stating rather than quietly fixing.

6 enzymes have an oligomer-level constant, not two. NylB and NylC carry theirs on 6-aminohexanoate dimer and cyclic oligomer. The other four are on genuine PET and PHA intermediates: 606, IsPETase and BhrPETase on BHET and MHET, which are the real intermediates of PET hydrolysis rather than chromogenic stand-ins, and PhaY1 on linear and cyclic 3-hydroxybutyrate oligomers. So the oligomer tier is wider than this page first reported, and two materials reach it rather than one.

And they fail anyway, which is the part that did not change. The polyamide constants cluster between 0.29 and 1.17 s−1, and at that order of magnitude surface erosion under body conditions comes out at a fraction of a micrometre per day — below the threshold at which erosion means anything. An 8 mm³ fragment is still there in decades. A previous draft quoted a single 0.77 s−1 for NylB, which is not one of its four measurements; the range is what the warehouse holds, and the conclusion survives being stated as a range.

This is the most useful result in the review. The material with the best evidence is measured to be too slow, and the materials with workable rates have no evidence that the rates are real. That is not a dead end — it is a specification. Either raise the rate on a route whose evidence already holds, or obtain polymer-level measurements on a route whose rate already looks workable. Both are concrete wet-lab programmes, and the second is cheaper.

What the degradation products do

Mechanism, not classification

Hazard mechanisms are kept deliberately separate from GHS codes. A regulatory classification has to be fetched from a registry and must never be hand-entered; a mechanism can be reasoned about and checked, and it is what tells a reviewer why a product matters. Both are tracked, and the system reports unresolved GHS status as an unanswered question rather than as a finding of safety.

Free iron — Fenton chemistry

Blocking · established · metal route

Liberated Fe(II) reacts with hydrogen peroxide to produce the hydroxyl radical, the most reactive oxygen species in biology, which reacts at diffusion-limited rates with whatever it meets:

Fe(II) + H₂O₂ → Fe(III) + OH⁻ + •OH

A wound is the worst possible setting for this reaction, because the co-reactant is already there and abundant: activated neutrophils generate peroxide at the site as part of the respiratory burst. The consequences are lipid peroxidation, protein carbonylation and DNA strand breaks in the tissue immediately around the fragment, plus iron overload as a known driver of ferroptotic cell death.

Requires: chelation-grade capture, not merely "capture". The gel has to bind iron as it is liberated and hold it redox-inactive — a chelator that retains iron but leaves it free to cycle between Fe(II) and Fe(III) does not solve this. This is the mechanistic reason the metal route is bounded by capture and not by rate: dissolving the fragment faster than the gel can sequester the ions converts a locally inert object into distributed oxidative injury, which is unambiguously worse than leaving it in place.

Bisphenol A — receptor agonism

Blocking · established · polycarbonate

Binds nuclear and membrane oestrogen receptors as an agonist at low concentrations. The dose-response is contested and non-monotonic in parts of the literature, which makes a "safe local amount" argument hard to make convincingly.

Requires: a specific regulatory and toxicological case before this product is generated inside tissue at all. The polycarbonate route is gated on it, not merely cautioned — which is why PC appears above with a workable candidate and a blocking verdict.

Aromatic diamines — genotoxicity

Blocking · established · polyurethane

Polyurethane hydrolysis can liberate aromatic diamines from the hard segment. Several, including 4,4′-methylenedianiline and the toluenediamines, are classified as human or probable human carcinogens.

Requires: a demonstrated amine-free product panel. For this route the product panel is the gate, not the kinetics — a polyurethane candidate must not advance on erosion rate alone.

Ethylene glycol — oxidative metabolism to oxalate

Serious · established · all polyester routes

Oxidised in vivo by alcohol dehydrogenase to glycolaldehyde, then glycolic acid, then oxalic acid. Oxalate precipitates with calcium as insoluble crystals, giving metabolic acidosis and calcium-oxalate nephropathy. The effect is systemic and dose-dependent rather than local.

It is also the hardest product to contain. At 62 g/mol its derived molecular radius is 0.26 nm — the smallest of any organic product here — so it diffuses out through a 20 nm gel mesh far more readily than the larger products do. In simulation it reaches 43% capture where cellobiose at 0.51 nm holds at 58%.

Requires: a bounded total release, not just a release rate. The absolute quantity is fixed by the fragment's mass through the mass balance, so for a small fragment it may fall far below any toxic threshold — but that has to be computed and stated rather than assumed. This is the strongest single reason the polyester route needs its mass balance closed.

1,4-butanediol — metabolism to GHB

Serious · established · PBS

Metabolised by alcohol and aldehyde dehydrogenase to γ-hydroxybutyrate, a controlled substance with recognised sedative and anaesthetic effects. Of every product considered here this is the one most likely to have a systemic effect at a small absolute amount.

Requires: capture, or a demonstrated release quantity far below a pharmacologically active dose.

Detached particulate — the failure mode the whole approach exists to avoid

Blocking · established · every route

Debris detached from the fragment surface is phagocytosed, drives a foreign-body response, and — being mobile — migrates away from the treated site. One locatable object becomes many unlocatable ones.

Requires: surface erosion rather than bulk fragmentation. This is why the model is a shrinking-core surface model and why the fragmentation gate exists: a route that breaks the fragment up has produced an outcome worse than the untreated state.

The clean panel: cellulose and the polyhydroxyalkanoates

Clear · established

Cellulose hydrolysis gives cellobiose and glucose. PHA gives 3-hydroxybutyrate, a physiological ketone body. PLA gives lactate, cleared by the Cori cycle. PBS gives succinate, a citric-acid-cycle intermediate. Polycarbonate's second product, carbonate, enters the bicarbonate buffer system.

Note what this means for the wood-first argument. The concept brief leads with wood because the product profile is the mildest of any material, and on the product side that argument is correct and survives this review intact. It is the kinetic side that has no support: the knowledge base contains no cellulase at all, so there is currently no candidate to put behind it.

The biological sequence

What the tissue is already doing, and where the intervention lands

The fragment is not sitting in neutral buffer. By the time anyone considers treating a chronic retained fragment, the tissue has completed a months-long response to it, and that response determines both what the enzyme can reach and what the host does in reply.

  1. Minutes

    Protein adsorption

    Plasma proteins including fibrinogen adsorb onto the fragment's surface and change conformation. This is the signal that recruits the immune system — and it is also the first obstacle to catalysis, because the surface the enzyme must reach is already coated.

  2. Hours to days

    Neutrophil and M1 macrophage recruitment

    Inflammatory cells arrive and attempt phagocytosis, secreting an acidic microenvironment and inflammatory cytokines. The respiratory burst generates hydrogen peroxide — the co-reactant that makes free iron dangerous at this specific site.

  3. Weeks

    Frustrated phagocytosis

    Unable to engulf an object orders of magnitude larger than themselves, macrophages fuse into foreign-body giant cells that attach to the surface and secrete degradative enzymes into a hypoxic local environment.

  4. Months

    Fibrotic capsule

    Fibroblasts lay down an avascular collagen wall; contractile myofibroblasts tighten it around the fragment, producing the mechanical pressure on adjacent nerves that generates the symptoms. The capsule also isolates the fragment — which limits migration, and limits access.

What the intervention has to survive

  • A coated surface. The protein corona reduces accessible surface area. The simulation treats accessibility as the binding constraint for crystalline materials, with values of 0.2–0.45 rather than 1.
  • Host proteases. The same proteolytic environment that makes a protease candidate dangerous also degrades the therapeutic enzyme. No half-life has been measured for any enzyme in this dataset under these conditions — the simulator reports that as an assumed parameter rather than substituting a plausible value.
  • 37 °C and pH 7.4. Nine enzymes in the dataset have temperature optima at or above 60 °C, and the strongest PET evidence sits at 50–70 °C on amorphous film at pH 8–9. Body conditions are not where these constants were measured.
  • Non-sterile fragments. A wood splinter carries soil and bacteria, and biofilm blocks the surface the enzyme is trying to reach.
  • An immune system with history. Eighteen enzymes in the dataset come from genera associated with wound infection, including three from Clostridium botulinum. A recombinant protein is not its source organism, but it may be a protein the local immune system is already primed against.

Where this leaves the project

Four conclusions

  • The enzyme has to be screened separately from the reaction. Five candidates in the dataset would act on host tissue, all of them offered for the same material, and a reaction-level bond-class check cleared every one. That screen is now a gate, host-unsafe candidates sort last at retrieval, and an unscreened enzyme fails too — because not asking is not the same as a negative answer.
  • Capture, not rate, is the binding constraint on the metal route. Fenton chemistry in a peroxide-rich wound is the mechanism, and it means a faster iron route is a worse one unless chelation keeps pace. This supports the mentor's caution about the metal direction on mechanistic grounds rather than market ones.
  • The wood-first argument survives on products and fails on kinetics. Glucose and cellobiose are the mildest product panel available, and that part of the brief holds. But there is no cellulase in the knowledge base, so the route currently has no candidate behind it. The gap is reported rather than filled with a constant borrowed from a polyester.
  • The decisive experiment is a measurement, not a design. Every refusal in this review traces back to the same absence: no turnover number on a solid fragment at body conditions. One such measurement, on a route whose products are already clean, would move more than another round of candidate generation.

Research prototype. Not a medical device, not a clinical decision aid, and not validated for use in or on a person. Every figure here is simulation output or a literature value, labelled with its provenance; nothing has been validated in vitro, in vivo or clinically.

The host-safety screen is built on enzyme classification. Class membership is not proof of harm in situ — a tethered enzyme inside a hydrogel is not the same exposure as free enzyme in tissue. The burden of proof is placed deliberately: a protease proposed for injection next to a nerve should have to demonstrate that it does not digest tissue, rather than be assumed safe because nobody checked.
